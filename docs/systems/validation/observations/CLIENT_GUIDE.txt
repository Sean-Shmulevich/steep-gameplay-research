VIDEO OBSERVATIONS AND CALIBRATION

240 rows from 11 measurement tables retain reported values, uncertainty, units, methods and video-segment identifiers where supplied. These are imported observations, predominantly from gameplay video. Footage was not remeasured in this pass. producer_status describes the source team's assessment, not independent verification here.

For numeric symmetric error rows, nominal_interval returns [value-error,value+error]. This is not automatically a statistical confidence interval. Qualitative entries and bounds must not be coerced into this form. chord_speed_floor computes sum(segment distances)/elapsed time; unsampled curvature makes that a geometric lower bound under accurate scale and timing.

Examples: the race estimate is 18.9 +/- 2 m/s and includes a reported 1.15 path-weave correction. The ski spin observation is 626 +/- 30 degrees/s derived from labelled rotation over airtime; it is not an input cap or proof of world-axis angular velocity. Reported camera FOV estimates depend on projection assumptions and do not establish every mode's FOV convention.

Use observations to compare output trajectories and timing, while preserving uncertainty in terrain registration, input, camera and playback. Do not replace traced formulas with a fitted constant merely because one clip matches. The old 7/12 m/s blend interpretation conflicts with the existing traced projected-speed conversion by 3.6. Test units explicitly. No observational row proves an internal transition, torque equation or contact solver.

Run python3 reference.py for worked examples and python3 test_reference.py for checks.

SNOW, INPUT AND RIDER REFERENCE

This folder is usable offline with ordinary Python 3. Run `python3 reference.py`
for worked examples. named_settings.json contains all 12 decoded simulation
profiles and all 10 decoded input profiles, with complete sampled curves. Values
in worked_examples.json are synthetic unless explicitly identified below.

Documented equations are implemented, rather than references to other files.
This is not a complete replacement physics engine: some external material
bindings and activity-specific control producers remain unspecified. See
snow/materials for the included environmental profiles. Those
inputs are explicit parameters. 

Conventions and distinct quantities

World up is (0,0,1). n_plane is the environmental material surface normal;
n_face is a hull/foil contact face normal. They are different: the bottom face
of a level hull can point downward while the material plane points upward.
V_total is geometric tetrahedral volume. W_total is the sum of eligible contact
face weights. These are different denominators. Total mass is main rigid-body
mass plus physical rider-body mass when that separate body exists.

Runtime speed is obtained from a body velocity with its reference-up component
removed. Input preset selection converts that magnitude by 3.6. This is
consistent with metres/second to kilometres/hour, but the documented equations
should retain the actual conversion rather than silently replacing the units.
Geometry is in runtime world-distance units; density is mass per cubed distance;
frequency-strength coefficients behave as rates with a 4*pi*pi conversion.
Angles used for buttering normalization are radians. Incidence-curve inputs are
normalized signed angles, with -1 and +1 representing -90 and +90 degrees.

Connected timestep and material binding

1. Snapshot rigid-body pose and velocities; transform the hull tetrahedra and
   foil geometry into world space. Append the current reference direction to a
   64-slot timestamped history and advance its timestamp by dt.
2. Query the environment at contact samples. A hit's collision material resolves
   a fluid-characteristics resource. That resource has a decoded curve profile,
   nominal layer range, and nominal layer thickness. The chosen plane retains
   that profile and thickness. A missing profile is finalized as zero layer data.
3. Ordinary hit adjustment moves the intersection along the query direction by
   normal.z*nominalThickness/dot(normal,queryDirection), then constructs the
   plane from the adjusted point. An alternate branch adds thickness vertically.
   A special layer-gap path clamps the observed gap to at least 0.001 and uses
   effectiveThickness=gap*nominalThickness/nominalRange.
4. Clip tetrahedra against the selected planes. Accumulate submerged volume,
   volume-weighted depth and surface normals. Update contact/air timers.
5. Cook input state. Supply material controls: cooked leg coil; clamped absolute
   buttering angle divided by pi/2; absolute cooked horizontal steering.
6. Update rider leg/contact targets and constraint state. Evaluate contact and
   density reactions for hull and foil geometry. Compute ground-stick impulses
   when its contact and activity gates allow them.
7. Apply density reactions and contact forces as point impulses to bodies.
   A scalar rider spring is integrated only when no separate physical rider body
   exists and configured rider mass is positive. A separate rider body instead
   receives its constraint/body path. These are alternative representations.

The world/camera forward vector supplied by the outer activity step is a
separate directional input. It is not the three-element material control array.
The selected material curves follow the collision material at the hit; they
are not selected from an equipment texture or visual snow label. Actual numeric
fluid profiles are now decoded in snow/materials, including groomed snow and
powder profiles with authored density 1000. Some external material references
remain unresolved. hullVolumicMass=600 belongs to the equipment hull and is not
the environmental snow density.

Geometry and depth

Classify a vertex p as wet when phi(p)=dot(n_plane,p)+d <= 0. For a crossing edge
from a to b, q=a+t*(b-a), t=-phi(a)/dot(n_plane,b-a). The intersection is not
clamped: the wet/dry classification selects a crossing edge. The near-parallel
tolerance is 0.00051; a near-plane endpoint may be returned instead.

A tetrahedron has volume abs(dot(b-a,cross(c-a,d-a)))/6 and centroid
(a+b+c+d)/4. Combine pieces with centroid=sum(V_i*C_i)/sum(V_i).
One wet vertex retains the tetrahedron formed by it and the three crossing
points. Three wet vertices retain the full tetrahedron minus the dry corner.
For two wet A,B and dry C,D, construct E=intersect(C,A), F=intersect(A,D),
G=intersect(D,B), H=intersect(B,C), then retain tetrahedra (A,B,E,G),
(F,A,E,G), (H,B,E,G). The reference implements all cases.

Immersion=sum(submerged volumes)/V_total. Values <=0.001 are zeroed. The ground
contact timer increases by dt when immersion>0.001 and resets otherwise; the
air timer tracks the complementary condition.

For a submerged application point, vertical depth fraction is:
  thickness <=0: 1
  abs(n_plane.z)>0.00051: clamp(-phi(point)/n_plane.z/thickness,0,1)
  otherwise: 0
Weighted depth=sum(V_sub*depthFraction)/sum(V_sub). This is not immersion.
Attitude settings select DepthRatio in the extracted surfboard profile, so do
not substitute total submerged volume ratio for its depth interpretation.

Density reaction and named simulation curves

P = fluid.volumicMass * V_sub * buoyancyDepthCurve(depthFraction) * n_plane
J_pressure = P * buoyancyAttitudeCurve(tiltInput)
               * buoyancySpeedCurve(projectedBodySpeed)
               * abs(worldGravity.z) * pressureActivation * dt

Gravity comes from the physics world. The algorithm uses absolute Z, not the
length of an arbitrary gravity vector. Tilt input is the normalized-asin lookup
of a clamped pose-matrix component. The speed input is cached body linear speed projected off the runtime reference-up
direction: length(bodyVelocity-referenceUp*dot(bodyVelocity,referenceUp)). It is
the same runtime speed used for input preset blending, without the 3.6 conversion.
The reference accepts the evaluated speed gain so callers can select their actual
profile; the complete curve data are included in named_settings.json.
J_pressure is applied at the submerged-volume application point.

Material coefficient selection and inputs

Each coefficient record has these eight sampled curves and baseMultiplier:
  flowAngleCurve, legCoilCurve, butteringCurve, steeringCurve,
  normalSpeedCurve, tangentialSpeedCurve, surfaceAngleCurve, depthCurve.
Each curve's output is clamped nonnegative before the gain product. The product
is clamped nonnegative and multiplied by baseMultiplier. A sign is optionally
attached to the normal result, but its square is used in force strength.

Relative point velocity is
  v = bodyLinearVelocity + cross(bodyAngularVelocity,point-centerOfMass)
                          - materialLinearVelocity
vn=dot(n_face,v), vt=v-vn*n_face, speed=length(v), tangentSpeed=length(vt).
Let L(x) denote the normalized signed-asin table. For nonzero speed:
  flowAngle = L(-clamp(vn/speed,-1,1))
  normalSpeed = abs(vn)
  tangentialSpeed = length(vt)
  normalSurfaceAngle = -L(clamp(dot(n_face,n_plane)*sign(vn),-1,1))
  tangentSurfaceAngle = L(clamp(dot(-vt/length(vt),n_plane),-1,1))
Use zero for the last input when tangential speed is zero.

Normal response selects hullPush or hullPull for hull contacts, foilPush or
foilPull for foil contacts. Negative flow selects Push; nonnegative flow Pull.
Tangential response selects hullDrag or foilDrag. Each normal/drag record uses
its corresponding surfaceAngle input; both use depth and the same controls:
  legCoilCurve input = cooked leg coil
  butteringCurve input = clamp(abs(butteringAngle)/(pi/2),0,1)
  steeringCurve input = abs(cooked horizontal steering)
The sign of steering is removed. These inputs are not world-forward components.

Contact force and integration

m_eff = contactActivation * totalMass/W_total * retainedContactFaceWeight
K(m,f)=clamp(m*f*f*39.47842025756836,1.0000000116860974e-7,999999995904)
F_normal = -dt*K(m_eff,normalFrequency)*vn*n_face
Clamp its magnitude to m_eff*abs(vn)/dt. Tangential force is
F_tangent = -dt*K(m_eff,tangentFrequency)*vt, with no corresponding stopping cap.
These velocity terms require effective mass>0, depth>0, and relative speed>
0.0001; normal/tangent components also require their own magnitude>0.0001.

For a hull contact with positive surface frequency, add
F_surface=K(m_eff,surfaceFrequency)*n_face. This addition can remain active even
when the velocity response is zero. The face-normal sign determines its direction.
The final point impulse is J_contact=dt*(F_normal+F_tangent+F_surface).
Thus the uncapped velocity terms contain dt twice; surface attraction contains
dt once. Removing one dt to make it look like standard damping changes behavior.

Apply a point impulse with
  linearVelocity += J/mass
  angularVelocity += inverseWorldInertia * cross(point-centerOfMass,J)
This converts the force pipeline to rigid-body motion without mislabeling force
as acceleration. The reference implements both force and point-impulse APIs.

Ground stick and the corrected averaging fields

The extracted generic settings have speedDifferenceThreshold=0 and
speedAveragingDuration=0.75. The field mappings were independently checked.
The history averaging divisor and ground-stick positive gate use duration,
not the zero difference threshold. Zero difference threshold does not disable
this path or cause a division by zero. Its complete downstream semantic use
has not been established, so no extra behavior is assigned to it.

For each of up to 64 samples younger than duration, let
w=speedAveragingCurve(age/duration), N=sum(w*direction)/sum(w).
For tangential velocity relative to the reference-up plane, s=length(v_t),
t=normalize(v_t), side=normalize(cross(t,worldUp)), D=cross(side,t),
alignment=dot(t,normalize(N)). Require dot(referenceUp,N)>-0.6, usable direction,
and forceSpeedCurve(s)>0.1. The speed curve is a gate; its output is not
multiplied into the resulting impulse.

Ordinary speed envelope E is 2*s below 15, 2*(30-s) between 15 and 30, and 0
at or above 30. Two activity-controlled override flags keep E=30 above 15.
One also selects a supplied mode scale and contact timer=1. Otherwise timer
=min(groundTime*6,1). The semantic activity names of these flags remain unknown;
reference parameters mode_a/mode_b identify their functional effects only.

When a boost/buttering interval activates the boost branch:
 B=max(1,sqrt(1-min(elapsed/boostDuration,1))*boostPeak), otherwise B=1.
A=-legCoilCurve(coil)*alignment*forceDepthCurve(weightedDepth)*E*B*timer*modeScale
Require A<-0.1 and a material-eligibility predicate or the activity override.
Then deltaVelocity=D*A*dt*externalScale;
J_rider=riderMass*deltaVelocity, J_board=(totalMass-riderMass)*deltaVelocity.
The activity/contact gates include transition and pending-correction suppression;
the reference assumes those caller gates have already permitted the impulse.

Input shaping, sampled curves and orientation

presetWeight=clamp((runtimeSpeed*3.6-7)/(12-7),0,1). Blend a normal and fast
parameter as normal*(1-weight)+fast*weight. The documented cooker, curve and
axis-shaping functions are portable and accompany all sampled presets.

For raw input r, compress magnitude at cutoff c with excess weight e:
 shaped=sign(r)*(min(abs(r),c)+max(abs(r)-c,0)*e)
 shaped*=1+boostWeight*(strengthBoost-1); clamp to [-1,1].
Steering horizontal gain evaluates its attenuation curve at vertical input;
vertical gain evaluates its curve at horizontal input. Blend normal/fast gains,
apply to their respective axes, then continue through remap/cooking.

Cooker selects ToZero if abs(target)<=abs(current) or current*target<=0,
otherwise ToOne. Its movement limit is
 step=(1-expLookup(-dt*proportional))*abs(target-current)+dt*base
Move toward target by min(step,abs(target-current)); snap residual<0.0001.
The exp lookup uses 256 linear segments over [-10,0], with padded endpoints.
Leg-coil target and cooked leg coil are separate values. The extracted surfboard
binding names TriggerRight as the leg-coil trigger, with no button entries.

For samples y_0..y_n over [lo,hi], step=(hi-lo)/n. Segment lines use the
unshifted sample grid. Indexing uses the padded domain with
 epsilon=min((hi-lo)*0.0001,0.0001), scale=n/(hi-lo+2*epsilon),
 index=floor((clampedX-(lo-epsilon))*scale), clamped to 0..n-1.
This padded segment selection differs slightly from ordinary interpolation.

Roll target=runtimeRollAngle*rollAngleSpeedCurve(speed)
                              *rollAngleImmersionCurve(selectedImmersion).
Yaw has its corresponding yaw curves. The selected immersion interpretation
must be respected; upstream activity definitions of every runtime angle are
not fully documented. The reference accepts the angle explicitly.

The incidence lookup has 256 segments with scalar target function
L(x)=(2/pi)*asin(x), x in [-1,1]. Construction uses float32 operations, a padded
lower limit about -1.00100005 and index scale about 127.872139. It is neither
acos nor a direct radian value. The reference uses standard asin. Vectorized approximations can
round differently, so bit-identical output is not guaranteed.

Rider spring and actual settings

When no physical rider body exists and rider mass is positive, integrate
 a=(stiffness*(targetPosition-position)+damping*(targetVelocity-velocity))/mass
   +externalAcceleration
 stiffness=K(mass,effectiveFrequency)
 damping=2*dampingRatio*sqrt(mass*stiffness)
using 10 RK4 substeps dt/10. Advance targetPosition by targetVelocity*substep
after each substep. The contact/leg setup precedes this integration and supplies
effective frequency, damping ratio and targets; its full nonlinear target
mapping is not yet documented. The supplied code therefore requires them as
inputs. It does not equate a configured base spring rate with every effective
contact rate, and it does not run the scalar model alongside a physical rider.

All 12 supplied rider profiles have mass=20. The surfboard gameplay hull has
hullVolumicMass=600, dynamic friction=0, restitution=0, and configured centre-of-
mass offsets x=0.045,y=-0.5. Its geometry and all other actual parameters are
included verbatim in named_settings.json. Only the material fluid profile is
missing; equipment density and material density must remain distinct.

Worked results and validation limits

At dt=1/60, synthetic density 1000, submerged volume 1/6, depth gain 1 and
vertical gravity magnitude 9.81, the pressure impulse is (0,0,27.25).
For synthetic totalMass=80, contactWeightSum=2 and retained weight=.5,
effectiveMass=20. Normal/tangent frequencies 2/1 and surface frequency .5 with
v=(2,0,-3), n_face=(0,0,-1) produce the complete force and impulse in
worked_examples.json. Controls coil .4, buttering pi/4, steering -.6 yield
(.4,.5,.6). Preset speeds 0,7/3.6,9.5/3.6,12/3.6 yield 0,0,.5,1.
Ordinary ground stick at speed 5, unit alignment and leg gain, depth gain .5,
fully active timer and dt=1/60 has A=-5 and deltaVelocity=(0,0,-1/12).
For riderMass=20,totalMass=80 its impulses are (0,0,-5/3),(0,0,-5).

Run python3 reference.py for the bundled examples. These checks cover local
calculations and data consistency. Full gameplay, animation and rendering
behavior require integration testing.
Curve sampling and arithmetic use mathematical formulas with normal floating-point rounding. One/three-wet tetrahedron decomposition uses equivalent geometry and still needs integration checks.
